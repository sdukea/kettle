The verdict

Don’t build the map. Build the blue dot.

“AI roadmaps for anything” is a free feature on the wrong problem. Maps won because your phone knows where you are. For almost every human goal, nothing does.

The insight

A goal is a test you can’t pass yet.

Take that literally and every hard question gets an answer you can check.

Destination

The arrival test

“Get an internship” becomes a concrete bar you can pass or fail.

Position

Measured, not guessed

Short, unaided checks. Never self-report, never hours watched.

Route

Cheap. Recomputed.

The shortest path from where you are, under your time and date.

Arrival

The real test

The interview or exam itself closes the loop and recalibrates everyone.

  1. Fix

    Fifteen minutes to find you.

    Four to six short checks. A coding problem, a bug to find, a concept to explain out loud.

  2. Skip

    It starts with what you can skip.

    The first result is time back. Then your two real gaps.

  3. Reroute

    Missed a week? It reroutes.

    No streak to break. A new ETA and the smallest useful session.

  4. Arrive

    It whistles when you’re ready.

    “You’re ready. Go apply.” A stop signal, backed by evidence. No content product has a reason to say it.

The name

Maps tell you where you are. A kettle tells you when it’s ready.

Kettle does both. It shows exactly where you stand on the route, gives you the next turn, and whistles when you’ve arrived. Until then it stays honest: still heating.

The psychological engine

Motivation that runs on true signals.

The test for every mechanism: would it still work if you fully understood it?

Uses

  • Mastery experiencesEarly checks are passable. Wins are measured, never awarded.
  • A real goal gradientThe distance to the arrival test shrinks only on evidence.
  • If-then planningEvery session ends with one tap: when and where next. d ≈ 0.65 across 94 studies.
  • Fresh startsAfter a lapse: a new week, a new route, no mention of the gap.

Refuses

  • Streaks you can loseLoss framing keeps people through fear.
  • Illusory progressBonus stamps work. We still won’t fake a head start.
  • Progress for time spentHours watched are not position.
  • Guilt notificationsAt most one a day, only for a session you chose.

The first user

Start where arrival has a date.

Early-career engineers with a technical hiring event 4 to 16 weeks away. The test is external, code can be graded automatically, cohorts are dense, and people already pay. We compete only on position, never on content.

The moat

When we say 70%, 70% pass.

Anyone can generate a plan. Only a company that has watched thousands of people go from measured position to real outcome can make that promise and keep it.

What to build first

Prove people will be measured.

Then prove the measurement predicts the real test. Each stage has to earn the next.

v0 · Concierge

No code.

Weeks 1–3

15 to 20 students with real dates. Position fixes by hand, a spreadsheet model, daily “Now” by message.

Stop if under 50% finish the first fix or under 30% return for week two.
v1 · One domain

The web app.

Weeks 4–14

Probe bank, code runner, explain-aloud checks, learner model, route solver, “Now”, readiness range.

No chat home, no calendar, no social, no content library.
V2 · Loops

Peers and passers.

Months 4–9

Peer checks, cohort seasons, arrival tests published by people who passed, plateau detection.

First internal calibration report.
V3 · Platform

More destinations.

Months 9–24

Adjacent roles on a shared graph, placement cells with student-controlled sharing, a position API.

Still no “any goal” intake.

What could break it

The biggest risk is human.

People avoid being measured when the answer threatens who they think they are.

People won’t take the checksFramed as skipping, kept private. The v0 kill criteria test this first.

Kill this idea

One idea dies here. One survives.

Killed

AI roadmaps for anything

A commodity feature on a problem that isn’t the bottleneck, with no position signal and no moat. The labs absorb it within a release cycle.

Kept

The GPS for one dated goal

Measurement first, honest about readiness, and defended by outcome data. If people won’t be measured, this dies too, and the next idea is making self-measurement feel safe.
