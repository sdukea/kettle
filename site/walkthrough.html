Free and paid

  • Free Set a goal, see the finish line, take the 15-minute check, get the full result (skip list, gaps, ready-by), see the route.
  • ₹499 season Daily tasks, weekly check-ins, the updating date, rerouting, timed mocks, and the “ready” call. Paid once, until test day.

Notifications

  • At most one a day: the reminder for the session the student chose.
  • One message after 3 quiet days. Then silence.
  • The weekly check-in reminder.
  • Never: streak warnings, “you’re falling behind”, or comparisons with others.

What Pillow stores

  • The goal, date, hours and language.
  • Every check: answer, score, time, confidence before it.
  • Each topic’s status over time, and sessions done.
  • The real test result, if the student shares it.

What Pillow never does

  • Solve problems for the student, or give hints during checks.
  • Share anything with colleges or employers.
  • Move progress for time spent instead of proof.
  • Use streaks, leaderboards or guilt to keep people coming back.
  • Say “ready” without a passed mock.

Should we take it further?

The design holds together. Four questions only real students can answer.

Screens 4–8

Will they take the check?

Students tend to avoid being tested when the result might hurt. If most quit during the 15 minutes, nothing after it matters.

Pass if at least half of those who start finish the check.
Screen 9

Is the result right?

If the skip list or gaps feel wrong, trust is gone on the first screen that matters.

Pass if most students say the skip list and gaps match what they know.
Screens 11–14

Do they come back?

One useful result isn’t a product. The daily task and the weekly check-in have to pull them back.

Pass if at least 30% complete the second weekly check-in.
Screen 10

Will they pay?

Liking it isn’t paying for it. A real ₹499 payment is the only proof.

Pass if at least 5 students in the first group actually pay.

My read

It’s worth taking further. The app is small and clear: one goal, one check, one result, one task a day, one “ready”. Nothing on these screens is hard to build.

The risk isn’t building it. It’s the four questions above, and they’re about people, not code. So build the thinnest version of screens 1–13, put it in front of 15 to 20 students with real test dates, and check those four numbers after three weeks.
